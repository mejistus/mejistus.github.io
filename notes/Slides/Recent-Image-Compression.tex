Since learned codecs caught up with VVC in 2020, research has split four ways, each with two key papers: fewer bits at the same error (TCM, DCAE), real-looking images from a few hundred bytes (PerCo, StableCodec), decoders small enough for a CPU (Cool-Chic, C3), and images compressed for models rather than people (TransTIC, a transform-neck for multimodal LLMs). For each, the problem it solves and how. JPEG AI (2025) shows where the four meet.

\section{Real-looking images from a few hundred bytes}

% 1:10
\begin{frame}{Branch 2: exact or realistic, not both}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}
        \item At low rates the MSE-optimal decoder outputs the \textbf{average} $\mathbb E[x\mid\text{bits}]$: blurry.
        \item At a fixed rate, more realism costs more distortion \cite{blau2018,blau2019}.
        \item So: send \textbf{structure}, let diffusion \textbf{invent texture}.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{center}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1.3cm, y=1.1cm, font=\small, >={Stealth[length=1.6mm]}]
        \draw[->] (0,0) -- (4.2,0) node[below left] {distortion $D$};
        \draw[->] (0,0) -- (0,3.1) node[above right] {unrealism $P$};
        \draw[sig, very thick, domain=0.7:1.35] plot (\x, {3.787*(1.35-\x)^2});
        \draw[hot, very thick, domain=1.6:3.1] plot (\x, {1.156*(3.1-\x)^2});
        \fill[sig] (0.7,1.6) circle (2.2pt) node[above, font=\small] {high $R$};
        \fill[sig] (1.35,0) circle (2.2pt);
        \node[hot] at (2.75,1.1) {low $R$};
        \fill[hot] (1.6,2.6) circle (2.2pt) node[right, black, font=\footnotesize] {MSE-optimal: blurry};
        \fill[hot] (3.1,0) circle (2.2pt) node[above right, black, font=\footnotesize, align=left] {perfectly\\realistic};
      \end{tikzpicture}
      \end{center}
      {\small Schematic, after \cite{blau2019}: perfect realism at most doubles the MSE.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \medskip
  \alert{Generated, not transmitted}: it looks right, but may not be what the camera saw.
\end{frame}

  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem.} Decoded images hurt MLLM accuracy at low rates; the LLM is too big to train through.
    \item \textbf{Method} \cite{kao2025neck}. A 13M-parameter neck maps $\hat y$ into CLIP \cite{radford2021clip}, trained on CLIP's features alone.

  \end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{Four branches in one table}

  \begin{tabular}{llll}

    \midrule

  \bigskip
  Each branch \alert{moves a cost}: larger models (1), invented detail (2), slow encoding (3), no picture for people (4).

\begin{frame}{Open problems}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Trusting generated detail}: text and faces can be invented; metrics cannot tell sent from made up.
    \item \textbf{Fast encoding} for overfitted codecs: each image is a training run.
    \item \textbf{Decoding everywhere}: bit-exact on every device, and parallel.
    \item \textbf{One bitstream, many receivers}: people and models from the same bits.
    \item \textbf{Trend}: codecs built on pretrained latents (diffusion VAEs, CLIP).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
