Research papers in LaTeX, read as web pages.

Made for computer science and AI papers, and the blogs that discuss them. Give HaTeX the LaTeX source of a NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, ACL or AAAI paper and it renders in the browser as the paper it is: title and authors, two columns where the template has them, numbered equations, tables and figures, TikZ, algorithms, natbib citations from your .bib, and the appendix. No TeX installation, no build step, no PDF viewer.

What is HaTeX?

html + latex = hatex

HaTeX is a LaTeX renderer for research writing on the web, aimed at computer science and AI. Papers in these fields are written in a handful of conference templates, and HaTeX reads them as they are: the template's title block, its two columns, natbib citations, and the bibliography from your .bib or from the .bbl that comes with every arXiv source. A paper goes onto a blog or a project page straight from its source, without being rewritten for the web.

Readers get the paper, not a PDF: equations, figures, tables and theorems numbered as LaTeX numbers them, every reference and citation a link, text that can be searched and copied, and a layout that fits any screen, in two balanced columns when there is room and one on a phone. The same source keeps working for the rest of your writing: posts, notes and beamer slides.

The work happens in the reader's browser. HaTeX parses the .tex source into HTML, typesets the maths with KaTeX and compiles TikZ with real TeX running in WebAssembly, so there is nothing to install and nothing to build. If you'd rather ship plain HTML, the same parser runs in Node at build time.

It covers the LaTeX that papers use, and a command it doesn't know degrades to its text instead of breaking the page. Papers in the manual lists the templates and shows how to bring over an arXiv source.

Quick start

Load KaTeX (for the maths) and HaTeX, then render a source into an element:

<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/katex@0.16.9/dist/katex.min.css">
<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/katex@0.16.9/dist/katex.min.js"></script>
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://mejistus.github.io/hatex/lib/hatex.css">
<script src="https://mejistus.github.io/hatex/lib/hatex.js"></script>

<article id="paper"></article>
<script>
  fetch('paper.tex')
    .then(r => r.text())
    .then(tex => HaTeX.render('#paper', tex));
</script>

The source can be a whole document (\documentclass … \begin{document}) or just its body. The preamble is read for \newcommand, \definecolor, \newtheorem and \usetikzlibrary. Serve the page over HTTP, not file://, so the .tex file and TikZ SVGs can be fetched. For highlighted code, add Prism and its language components; don't add a Prism theme.

What it renders

AreaSupported
Structure\section … \subsubsection (numbered, * for unnumbered), \paragraph, abstract, nested itemize / enumerate / description, quote, center, \footnote
Maths (KaTeX)$…$, \(…\), \[…\]; equation, align, gather, multline, … numbered per row, \nonumber, \label / \eqref; \newcommand and \DeclareMathOperator
Tablestabular with \toprule / \midrule / \bottomrule, \hline, |, \multicolumn, \multirow, \cmidrule(lr), \rowcolor / \cellcolor; \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{…} shrinks a wide table to fit
Figures\includegraphics[width=…\linewidth], subfigure (a), (b), side-by-side minipage, \caption; click an image to zoom
TikZtikzpicture, tikzcd, pgfplots axis; libraries detected from the code; nn* styles for network diagrams
Theoremstheorem, lemma, proposition, definition, … and proof; your own with \newtheorem
Algorithmsalgorithm + algorithmic: \State, \If, \For, \While, \Repeat, \Function, \Comment, …
Codelstlisting, minted, verbatim, \verb; highlighted when Prism is loaded
Papersthe title block of \maketitle and ICML's \icmltitle (authors, affiliations, \thanks); the conference templates' own commands skipped quietly; the bibliography from a .bib or an arXiv .bbl
References\ref, \eqref, \autoref, \cref, \cite{a,b}, natbib's \citep / \citet as “(Vaswani et al., 2017)” / “Vaswani et al. (2017)”, thebibliography; each link scrolls to its target
Text\textbf, \emph, \texttt, \sout, \hl, \textcolor, \href, \url, sizes, accents, dashes and quotes
Chinesecaptions and names switch to 图 / 表 / 定理 / 证明 / 参考文献; no stray spaces at CJK line breaks; \begin{guji} sets vertical text on ruled sheets of 3:4 like the leaves of an old book (right to left, 句讀 marks in place of punctuation, one sheet per printed page) and \jiazhu{…} adds two-line interlinear notes (夾注)
Layout\documentclass[twocolumn], the two-column conference styles (ICML, CVPR, ACL, AAAI, IJCAI, IEEE, ACM) and multicols: balanced columns on a wide screen, one column on a phone; headings, the abstract and figure* / table* span both columns, and equations too wide for a column are scaled to fit
Slides\documentclass{beamer}: frame, \titlepage, \tableofcontents, block / alertblock / exampleblock, columns; any aspect ratio (16:9, 4:3, 1:1, …); a full-screen Present mode; overlays (\pause, <2->) show every step at once
